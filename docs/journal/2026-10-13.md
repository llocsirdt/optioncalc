## Backtests are directional, not literal

**Realization:** live v7-10 has had about one positive day in seven (that one about +$40), with floors mostly below −$1k and often
near −$1.5k. The backtest tables said 80% wins, now 60%. Live doesn't come close to the tables.

- From now on, a backtest is evidence that a change isn't *devastating*. It is not a forecast. Dollar figures in sweep tables are
  read as direction only.
- **Decision:** deploy the plateau cover on v7-10 anyway: offset1 condor cover, minLock 0.05, and give-ups move back to the tent.
  The backtest of that exact combination said "a little worse" (−$578 vs −$492 closing, max drawdown better at −$7,433 vs −$9,985).
  It gets judged on live results. Deployed as `ddaa612`.

## What a strategy should look like

- The user rarely trades 10W in real life. 20W gives a 40-point natural tent and a strike inside the open to cover more cheaply
  when wrong.
- The goal is a risk curve shaped like **one wide peak or plateau**, not a row of narrow 10W peaks and troughs. 10W peaks rarely
  overlap, because the leg ledger slots each strike as buy-only or sell-only.
- **How the user actually trades:** in any moment they may use one strategy's open and another's cover. The choice depends on where
  price sits relative to recent candles, the moving averages and the Bollinger bands, and on whether they're building peaks or
  raising the floor.
  - Price just crossed one MA and closed between it and the next → the next MA is the target. Be patient and cover wide.
  - Price boxed between two tight MAs → open and cover quickly inside that range.
- NDX's own MAs and bands are a secondary check on the /NQ signal.

## Floor raises — the day's big lesson

1. **Same spread, three times.** v7-10 bought C30580/30590 at 13:00 (×2, $1.95 each) and 13:45 ($1.65), on the *high-profit side*
   of a book already locked in profit (that area at +$1,331 / +$2,171 while the true floor sat at +$331 / +$171). Each premium
   *lowered* the floor it was meant to raise. Cause: once every low area is above zero, the planner may "lock in more profit" on
   any of them, without checking that the actual floor rises. Two per pass is by design.
2. **Against the market.** 13:00 was a falling 15-minute bar and it bought a call spread above the market. The trend check
   (`floorRaiseTrend`) had existed since 10-07 and was never turned on.
3. **Far out with no extra reward demanded.** 3:1 everywhere. The setting to require more further out existed, also never on.
4. **Overpaid.** $1.65 filled in 6 seconds against a $1.55 mid and a $3.10 ask. The market took the top of our range.
5. **Most of the menu is thrown out every pass.** The quote check rejected 200–320 structures per pass. Every single long option
   is rejected (its maximum payoff is measured one point past the strike), and cheap out-of-the-money spreads are rejected as "too
   cheap" against a deliberately doubled expected move. → after the session.
6. **The user's condor.** At 14:20 the floor's low spot was −$244 at 30530–30540, with NDX at 30531. Every all-call and all-put
   condor or butterfly over it conflicted with the leg ledger: the account was short C30520 and long P30540. The iron version —
   long P30520 / short P30530 / short C30540 / long C30550 — is legal on every leg and pays the same shape. The menu had never
   offered put/call mixes. **The user saw it, placed it by hand, and halted the engine to lock in the day's profit.** That is the
   clearest example yet of the user's judgment beating the engine, and the reason for the review loop on the backlog.

**Built today** (`5c2114f` + the iron mix; not deployed at time of writing):
- A raise must lift the real floor once the book is in profit (`floorRaiseLockedLift`).
- Far ratio: 3:1 at the money, rising to 6:1 two expected moves out (`floorRaiseMinRatioFar` 6).
- Walk raise orders up: start at 0.4 × mid, +$0.05 every 20 s, up to the approved price (`floorRaiseWalk`, live only).
- Put/call (iron) mix for any butterfly or condor whose own legs the ledger refuses (`floorRaiseIronMix`, live only). The test
  reproduces the user's exact order.
- **Trend check: built, swept, and turned back off.** With it on, the closing floor was worse on 4 of 5 strategies (v7-10 −613 →
  −717; v7-20 −484 → −560; v9-20 −483 → −500; v4-20 −423 → −485; v7-40 −1,149 → −1,138). The user's intuition ("we should have held
  off") is right about 13:00, but this definition of trend isn't the one that captures it. Open question.
- Sweep caveat: with all of today's rules, v7-10 scores worse than with the old ones (−578 → −717). On 20W/40W the far ratio and
  locked-lift rules help (v7-20 −591 → −484 without the trend check; v7-40 −1,340 → −1,149). Directional only, per the morning's
  lesson. The walk-up and the iron mix aren't in the backtest at all.

## Late afternoon — hedge prices and where hedges may go

- **The real price of the user's condor:** filled at an $8.20 credit at 14:32, a debit-equivalent of $1.80. It cost $180 and lifted
  30530–30540 by about $820 (≈4.6:1). (The $1.30 the new code produced in its test came from a model-priced chain, not real quotes.)
- **Hedge orders must start notably low and walk up.** The walk starts at 0.4 × mid, and now never goes above the mid itself (it
  was mid + 2 ticks). Today's overpayments filled the instant they were sent.
- **The trend check is really a question about payoff ratio by distance.** What ratio is acceptable for a hedge a given distance
  out of the money? The far-ratio setting (3:1 → 6:1 at two expected moves) is the first answer; the schedule itself is still to
  be decided.
- **Where hedges may go (user's rule, built):** a butterfly or condor out past the book only moves the risk point beyond its own
  strikes. So any hedge with more than two legs must sit inside the strike range of the *real* positions (opens and covers). Out
  past that range, only clean two-leg spreads (or single longs) inside the expected move — the wing concept. The 30170 question
  was the right instinct even though no such order was found.

- **The range rule measured well:** better on all five strategies on both closing floor and locked days (v7-10 −613 → −591; v7-20
  −484 → −462; v7-40 −1,149 → −1,105; v9-20 −483 → −454; v4-20 −423 → −399). With it, v7-10 is back about level with the morning's
  rules (−578).
- **Baselines:** the put/call mix was ported into the backtest (shared `FR.ironTwin`) so the baseline doesn't block raises live can
  place. The walk-up stays live only (it only lowers the price paid). Full baseline rebuild started on the 978-day combined set.

- **New baselines (cba76fa, 821 days):** closing floor better on 74 of 80 strategies; the big gains are 20W/40W (cover range +
  raise rules), e.g. v7-40 −2,623 → −1,131, v9-20 −1,451 → −461. 10W slightly worse on most (v7-10 −492 → −582, locked 29.3% →
  26.5%), mostly the plateau cover chosen this morning.
- **Found in the rebuild:** v0, v1 and v2 are now identical at 20W and 40W. They differ only in cover geometry (tent / halfway /
  at-money), and the 20W/40W cover range replaces geometry, so three strategies collapsed into one. A roster question for the
  controls rework.

- **A known limit of the locked-floor rule, accepted for now:** with two equal low points and a positive floor, no single hedge can
  lift the real floor (raising one lowers the other by its premium), so the rule blocks them all, 2-leg wings included. The user
  prefers this to the morning's behaviour and will manually raise one low when needed so the engine re-engages on the other.
  On the backlog to explore (a tolerance around the floor, or judging a pass's two picks together) and to watch live.

## Evening — acting at the open, and always placing on a signal

- **09:30 is a timeframe alignment, not a strategy change.** The /NQ signal series runs 24h, so the 09:15–09:30 bar is complete at
  09:30. Live now acts at 09:30 and prices off NDX's first 1-minute close (~09:31); acting before that bar closes would have centred
  strikes on *yesterday's* close. NDX's 09:30 print isn't stale (0 of 69 days equal to the prior close), but it moves a median 14
  points in the first minute. Revert switch: `CANDLE_SPREAD_FIRST_ACTION=935`.
- **The combined backtest set was assembled by hand on 10-08:** 922 Kaggle /NQ days (24h, priced off NQ — no NDX before 2026)
  plus 56 NQ+NDX days from 2026. The 2026 files were rebuilt with a 09:30 bar priced from NDX's first minute.
- **"Always place an order on a signal" (user).** Opening quotes are chaotic, but a realistic price is known — about 50–55% of
  width for a bull open, 47–52% for a bear. If no placement can be priced, the engine now places anyway: short strike nearest the
  underlying, at the low end, walked up to the high end. The user extended it to the whole day: an unfavourable price is a reason
  to place low and work the order, never a reason to place nothing. After 09:30 the range is the variant's own band (10W
  $4.50 → $5.50). (Correction to an earlier claim: the 10W open cap is 55%, not 65%.)
- **Signal visibility, built the same evening.** The debug page's new Signals panel lists every trigger a strategy can open or
  cover on, at the candle under the time slider: fired / held by a gate / met but outranked / not met / off, with each condition
  and its value, plus the last trigger of the day. It re-states each family's rules (classic, v4, v5, v6, v7; v8 and v9 share v6
  and v7) and is proved against the real signals: 369,020 evaluations on real bars, 0 mismatches.

- **The 09:30 sweep (30 strategies, closing floor):** mixed, as a timing change should be. 10W mostly better at 09:30 (v7-10
  −582 → −553, v5-10 −373 → −312, v4-10 −390 → −356); several 20W/40W worse (v5-20 −249 → −336, v0/1/2-20 −169 → −200, v9-40

- **The backtest cannot see the opening fallback.** Its Black-Scholes chain never prices every placement over the cap at 09:30,
  so "fallback at the open" was identical to "no fallback" on all 30. The case for it is the live chain's chaos at the open,
  which the backtest doesn't model. Kept on the user's rule; judge it live (`open-fallback` decisions).
- **All-day fallback only fired on 10W in the backtest** (20W/40W identical), mostly slightly worse there (v7-10 −553 → −566).
